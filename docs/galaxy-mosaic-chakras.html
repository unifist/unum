Starting · a spiral out from the heart

How do you get started?

Whatever you're building, you start the same way: from a problem, spiraling out through seven facets, one question each.

Heart first, then one up, one down, two up, two down, three up, three down.

Starting · 1 of 7 · What's the problem?

Exception

When what you expected doesn't match what happened.
What it is
The pivot of the spine. The three facets below it are what you do; the three above it are what you believe. Any problem, anywhere, comes in through this one door and gets recorded.
The chainsaw story
Saturday arrives, and the chainsaw doesn't. That gap gets written down. It's an exception, and fixing it becomes work.
Compared to the heart chakra
The chakra
Anahata, "unstruck": the middle of the seven. Balance, compassion, the still center.
This facet
The middle of the spine, where every mismatch lands.
Alike
Both are the center, where the upper half and the lower half meet.
Different
The heart is unstruck. The exception is where reality strikes. It rings with dukkha, the friction of things not being right, until every facet is whole.
Starting · 2 of 7 · What did you expect?

Expectation

What should happen, said out loud.
What it is
A prediction you can check. It's stated up front so reality has something to be compared against. Without one, there's nothing to notice when things go wrong.
The chainsaw story
"I'll have the chainsaw Saturday morning." That sentence is what made the empty driveway on Saturday an exception.
Compared to the throat chakra
The chakra
Vishuddha: expression and speaking truth. The voice.
This facet
Saying what should be.
Alike
Both are the voice: putting what's inside into words others can hear.
Different
The throat speaks truth. An expectation can turn out wrong, and that's the point: it's built to be checked.
Starting · 3 of 7 · What can you do about it?

Acception

One action, actually taken.
What it is
The smallest unit of doing. Each one is recorded, so what actually happened can be seen and counted, not just what was planned.
The chainsaw story
Call the holder and pick it up today. That single action is an acception.
Compared to the solar plexus
The chakra
Manipura: fire. Will and power, turning intention into doing.
This facet
The doing itself, one act at a time.
Alike
Both are where will becomes action.
Different
The solar plexus is personal power. An acception is a single act the whole group can see.
Starting · 4 of 7 · What do you need to track?

Enumeration

Naming and cataloging the things that matter.
What it is
The group's shared vocabulary: its terms. A term starts as a plain description and sharpens into exact details only as people need them to act.
The chainsaw story
"Chainsaw," "reservation," "due date." Everyone means the same thing by each word, and each one is tracked. That's what makes the expectation checkable.
Compared to the third eye
The chakra
Ajna: insight, seeing clearly. Two sides become one view.
This facet
Discovering what's there and giving it a name.
Alike
Both are about seeing what's actually there.
Different
Insight is private. Enumeration is written down and shared, so everyone sees the same thing.
Starting · 5 of 7 · How does it fit?

Acceptation

A workflow: a set of actions held together, and what feeds into what.
What it is
Pipes and queues. One step's output is the next step's input. It's a story told as a workflow: who does what, in what order, and what happens next.
The chainsaw story
Borrowing the chainsaw is a workflow. Someone requests it, the holder gets a reminder with a due date, it's handed off, it's returned, and it's back on the shelf.
Compared to the sacral chakra
The chakra
Svadhisthana: water. Desire, pleasure, creativity, flow.
This facet
The channels work flows through.
Alike
Both are about flow: things moving, mixing, passing from one to the next.
Different
No counterpart for desire or pleasure. Acceptation carries the flow, not the wanting.
Starting · 6 of 7 · What would the commitments be?

Affirmation

A recorded commitment. Joining the whole.
What it is
What each member promises. You can't have an organization until people commit; affirmations are those commitments.
The chainsaw story
Each member affirms: "I'll answer a borrowing request within a day." Those promises are what the five neighbors hold each other to.
Compared to the crown chakra
The chakra
Sahasrara: union. The self joins the whole.
This facet
Committing to the group and joining it.
Alike
Both are joining something larger than yourself.
Different
It's union you can leave.
Starting · 7 of 7 · Who holds it together?

Organization

The group itself: the people who've committed, and what they own and run together.
What it is
An organization isn't there first. It's made of people who've committed to the same things. Once it exists, it holds up and supports each person who commits to it.
The chainsaw story
The five neighbors own the chainsaw together, share the upkeep, and run the lending.
Compared to the root chakra
The chakra
Muladhara: grounding and survival. The base that holds the body up.
This facet
The group that holds each member up.
Alike
Both are the foundation. You stand on them, and everything above depends on them.
Different
The root is your own body's base. An organization is built out of other people's commitments.
