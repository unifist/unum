Unifist Unum Logical Flow

Ground Up

Unifist Unum is based entirely on the ground up shit not working.

It's not based on some ideology. It's not based on some grand vision. It's based on working systems that immediately deliver value to both the organization (Unum) and its members (Unifist).

The first rule of Unifist Unum is everyone, including the org, should be getting back more than they're putting in.

No faith. No hope. No promises. It works out of the gate or we don't work at all.

Unifist Unum Logical Flow

The shit bucket principle

We start with the shit bucket principle, literally.

The bare bones of any organization is a list of things that aren't right and one person doing one process to make each right.

What we do to improve that process is separate specialized processes from that universal one.

We only do this if it is more efficient to do so.

Cumulus is the part that handles the shit bucket.

Handling the issues you can handle.

Scat

In Cumulus, an issue is called a Scat.

A funny word for shit, without swearing.

The goal of a Scat is to be Flushed.

Something you gotta deal with, and then you don't have to deal with it anymore.

A Flush can have one or more Scats.

Time

Once we Flush more than one Scat at once, that's a sign of efficiency.

Consider shopping for groceries. Now you could go to the store each time you need something.

Say the store is a 10 min round trip, 5 min there and 5 back, and it takes one minute to shop for one item.

Each time you run out of these three items it takes 33 min, or 11 min each.

But if we bundle the shopping, it takes 13 min, or 4 min each.

The evolve through failure principle

We act on failure to do, and to fail to do so, we so act on that failure to change.

If an organization cannot handle issues, they will pile up and continue to do so until they are handled.

We must add analysis of failures to our system, adapting to handle them.

Change must come from within.

Sparks

Sparks are Scats that can't be Flushed.

A Spark isn't a new Entity. It's just a Scat in the Spark Bucket.

In Unifist Unum, apps build on top of each other.

So the Lightning app installs its own Spark Bucket.

Sparks are how we first separate out the problematic Scats.

Unifist Unum Logical Flow

Bolts

From these Sparks we can create Bolts. Bolts are Expectations.

What's common to these Sparks? What Rule are they breaking?

These Rules inform how we expect the world to be.

They can function like a project when new.

After working, Bolts can function as laws, automatically generating work as soon as they're broken.

All Bolts immediately get a Bucket.

Unifist Unum Logical Flow

Bolts keep Sparks from massing

By starting to group them, Scats that still can't be Flushed don't turn into a giant mass.

With a Bucket for every Bolt, we can now route all those unflushable Scats to a Bucket, so they don't muck with anything else.

Strikes

Strikes are the actions we take.

A Strike is the record of actually doing something, assigned to an Entity.

Strikes can come from a Bolt, a Scat, or nothing at all.

Like tickets, Strikes can have parents, blockers, and related Strikes.

Unifist Unum Logical Flow

Strikes become Rods

Strikes start as ways to learn and work on Scats.

Once figured out, they get easier and easier.

Eventually we create Rods from Strikes. Rods generate Strikes.

Flushing a Bucket is a Rod.
